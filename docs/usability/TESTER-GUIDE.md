# EpiScope: a short test for you

Thank you for helping. EpiScope is a student project: a website that health officers in Bangladesh could use to
see where a disease is rising, how many cases to expect, and where to send supplies. All the numbers in it are
made up for practice. No real patient is in it.

We are testing the website, not you. If something is hard to find or hard to understand, that is exactly what we
want to learn. There are no wrong answers.

- **Time:** about 20 to 30 minutes.
- **You need:** a laptop or desktop computer (a phone screen is too small), and a way to write notes.
- **Please:** work alone and do not ask anyone for help. If you get stuck on a task for more than 3 minutes,
  write down where you got stuck and move on to the next task.

## Before you start

1. Open this link in Chrome, Edge or Firefox: **<DASHBOARD LINK>**
2. Wait until a map of Bangladesh appears. This can take up to half a minute.
3. At the top of the page you will see the title **EpiScope** and a row of boxes: **Disease**, **District**,
   **Alert method** and **Time range**. Leave **Alert method** and **Time range** as they are for the whole test.

## Task 1: Find this week's alerts for a district

An "alert" means the website thinks cases are rising faster than normal in a district.

1. Read the line just under the row of boxes. It starts with **On alert in the last full week**. Write down what
   it says.
2. Next to **Disease**, click **dengue**.
3. Click the **District** box and choose **Chattogram** from the list.
4. Find the panel called **Early warning ranking**. It is a table. The Chattogram row is now coloured blue.
5. In the Chattogram row, read the columns **Alert now** and **Alert weeks**.
6. Look at the map in the panel called **Cases per 100,000 people**. Triangles on the map mark districts with
   alerts. The key under the map explains a solid triangle and an outlined one.
   Chattogram has a thick black outline on the map.

**Answer:** Is Chattogram on alert this week for dengue? In how many weeks did it have an alert? Is any district
in the country on alert this week, for any disease?

## Task 2: Read a forecast and its range

A forecast is the website's guess for the coming weeks. The "likely range" is how far off the guess might be.

1. Keep **dengue** and **Chattogram** chosen.
2. Scroll down to the panel called **Weekly dengue cases: Chattogram**. It has a line chart of cases per week.
3. Under the chart there is a small table called **Forecast for Chattogram, made on** (and a date). It has three
   columns: **Week starting**, **Expected cases** and **Likely range**.

**Answer:** How many dengue cases does the website expect in the first week of the table? What is the lowest and
the highest number it thinks is likely? Look at the last row, **Next 4 weeks**: how many cases should the
hospitals prepare for over the four weeks?

## Task 3: Explain why a district got its supplies

The website suggests how to share a fixed amount of money for medical supplies between districts.

1. Scroll to the panel called **Resource allocation**.
2. Find the box called **Supply shown** and choose **1-litre bags of IV saline**.
3. Under the grey box, read the three lines in bold type: **All districts**, **All supplies** and **Chattogram**.
4. The **Chattogram** line says how many bags it needs, how many it has, how many it is short, and how many it
   receives.
5. Look at the bar chart below. Each district is one bar. The Chattogram bar has a dark outline. The key under the
   chart explains the colours: **in stock**, **sent by this plan** and **need, next 4 weeks (high estimate)**.

**Answer:** In your own words, why does Chattogram receive the number of saline bags it gets? Could you explain
it to someone who has not seen the website?

## Task 4: Try the "what if" controls

Inside the grey box at the top of **Resource allocation** there are sliders. You move a slider by dragging its dot
left or right. The number next to its name changes as you move it.

1. Keep **1-litre bags of IV saline** chosen.
2. Read the **All supplies** line. It says what share of the shortfall the plan covers. Write down the number.
3. Drag the **Budget for all supplies** slider all the way to the right. Read the **All supplies** line and the
   **Chattogram** line again.
4. Drag the same slider back to the middle.
5. Drag the slider **Minimum share for every district (equity floor α)** all the way to the right. Read the
   **All supplies** line again, and look at the small chart under the bars, called **Cost of a minimum share**.

**Answer:** What happened when you gave more money? What happened when you raised the minimum share for every
district? Did you understand what "minimum share for every district" means?

## Task 5: Read the policy brief

The policy brief is a two-page letter to the person in the Ministry who decides.

1. Open this link: **https://github.com/Shinzuu/episcope/blob/main/docs/EpiScope-report.pdf**
2. Go to page 10. On the right side you will find **Appendix A, Policy Brief for the Director**. It continues on
   page 11.
3. Read the brief. It should take about five minutes.

**Answer:** What does the brief ask the Director to decide? About how much money would cover every district's
shortfall? By when should supplies for dengue be in place in Dhaka division?
