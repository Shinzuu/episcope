\section{Limitations and Future Work}
The data is synthetic, so detection and forecast scores measure how well the methods recover patterns we
planted, not real-world accuracy. The storage layer runs on the local filesystem, not HDFS or an object store
(ING-5). Patient de-duplication is precise but misses most repeats whose age or sex was recorded differently.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Data.} Synthetic stock follows a simple min/max policy; real stock records would change the
allocation. Next: load a real stock feed through the same S6 contract.
\item \textbf{Detection.} The threshold was tuned on outbreaks we planted, and a district that stops reporting
raises no alert. Next: re-tune on reported outbreaks from DGHS bulletins, and add a reporting-gap alert.
\item \textbf{Forecasting.} The intervals ignore parameter uncertainty, and dengue forecasts tend to fall short
in a surge. The backtest reads the final counts, and malaria forecasts are not backtested. Next: a bootstrap
over the fit, a growth term, and scoring each origin on the as-of snapshot.
\item \textbf{Allocation.} Costs and units per case are rough assumptions, and the LP plans one four-week round.
Next: real prices and a rolling plan that carries stock forward.
\item \textbf{Dashboard and access.} Role bundles are not enforced by the static server. Next: a login with a
path per role.
\item \textbf{Scenarios.} The SEIR model is deterministic and national. Next: per-division fits with a range.
\end{itemize}
