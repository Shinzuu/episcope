\section{Dashboard Design and Usability}
The dashboard (\texttt{dashboard/}) uses only D3.js v7 and the TopoJSON client, both vendored with
subresource-integrity hashes. It loads only the aggregated JSON exports, which must stay under 10\,MB: the
export keeps the most recent weeks that fit. Five views are linked (Fig.~\ref{fig:dashboard}): a district choropleth of incidence per
100,000 with alert markers, a weekly time series with a brush, forecast and bands, a seasonal heatmap, an
allocation view with a what-if panel, and a ranked early-warning table. Suppressed cells are hatched and every
view shows the data-freshness time.
\textbf{Design rationale.} Incidence uses the viridis scale, which stays ordered in greyscale and for the
common colour-vision deficiencies; alerts use the Okabe-Ito vermillion, which stands out against it. Counts
are shown per 100,000 on the map, so a large district does not look worse only for being large, but as raw
cases on the time series, which is what a store-keeper plans with. Suppressed cells are hatched, not
left blank, so ``hidden'' never reads as ``zero''. Every forecast carries its 80\% band. District by week is
the finest level shown: it is what an officer acts on and the coarsest level that still locates an outbreak.
Below 560\,px the views stack in one column for a phone.

\textbf{What-if panel (VIS-5).} Three sliders recompute the views in the browser from precomputed results. The
budget and the minimum share per district pick one of the 55 allocation scenarios; the alert-level slider
multiplies each method's threshold from 0.5 to 2.0, so an officer sees how many alerts a stricter or looser
level would give before anyone changes the detector. The rule of at least five cases in four weeks still applies, recomputed from the
published counts only; for CUSUM the slider approximates, because the statistic resets at the default $h$.

\textbf{District view (PRV-3).} Opening the dashboard with \texttt{?district=} and a district code loads that
district's bundle: its own series, alerts, forecast and allocation in full, and the rest of the country drawn
lighter as division totals.

\textbf{Simulated persona walkthroughs.} We had no access to non-CS users in the final phase, so the
usability check was run as \emph{simulated persona walkthroughs with AI agents}: three AI agents, each briefed
as one persona, used the real dashboard of an SF=1 build of 21 October, before the allocation cap, through a scripted headless browser. They are
not human participants, and their findings are a design review, not user evidence. The personas were a
district health officer in Cox's Bazar using the district view, a hospital store-keeper in Chattogram, and a
senior ministry official. Each did five tasks: find this week's alerts, read a forecast with its range, explain
an allocation, use the what-if panel, and read the policy brief (protocol and records in
\texttt{docs/usability/}).

Round 1 found 48 problems. Nothing showed who was on alert \emph{now}: the map and the table counted alerts
over six months. The newest week, still incomplete, looked like a sudden fall. The four-week forecast was a
flat line with no numbers, and a threshold line drawn on the case axis contradicted the alerts. The allocation
used codes such as q90 and $\alpha$, gave no units or currency, and opened on a supply with nothing to
allocate. We added a line naming the districts on alert in the last full week, marked the partial
week, added a forecast table with a cautious four-week total, removed the threshold line, renamed every label
in plain words, and added a sentence explaining each district's allocation. In the confirm round the agents
rated 26 of the 48 problems fixed, 20 partly fixed and 2 not fixed. Round 2 logged 17 new problems, 13 of them distinct, two of high severity: the hollow alert markers were drawn
solid, and at the largest budget with $\alpha=1$ the plan sent 9,237 antimalarial courses against a national
shortfall of 684 (as the dashboard showed it then), because a full population split forces supplies to every district. We then fixed the
markers, called a cell hidden to protect another cell ``hidden'' instead of ``fewer than 5'', made the ranking
fit its panel, added a cautious four-week total and an all-supplies line, and said on screen that the plan does
not move stock between districts. These fixes were not tested in a third round. The over-allocation
was then removed by capping every allocation at need (Section~\ref{sec:alloc}). Still open: unlabelled budget lines in the equity chart, no
delivery week, and method names that remain partly technical.
